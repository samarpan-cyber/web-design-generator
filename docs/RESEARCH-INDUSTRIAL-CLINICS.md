# Industrial and clinic design research (version 8)

Research was done with web searches and by reading two real prospect sites end to end. The search results are agency and vendor blogs, so treat their claims as informed opinion, not measured results. Below: what was found, and what we did with it.

## Two real sites used as test subjects
- **panchal-plastic.com** (plastic recycling machinery, Gujarat). About 41 products with images, categories such as extruders, shredders, agglomerators, grinders, washing lines, mixers, an ISO 9001 mention, a phone and address. Before this version it was detected as electronics and the name came out as the URL. Now it is detected as **plastic recycling, machinery maker**, the name falls back to the host, and the products and certification are shown.
- **eliteaquachem.com** (pickling, passivation, degreasing and polishing chemicals for stainless steel). About 20 products, an "Industries we serve" card grid with photos, quality cards (batch traceability, ISO 9001:2015 and others), a client logo wall. Now detected as **chemicals, metal and surface treatment**, with the industries cards shown as photo tiles and the quality cards as a standards section.

## What the industrial and manufacturing guides agree on
- **The quote request is the conversion point.** Quantity fields, several products in one request, a short form. Every industrial design has an RFQ basket (add products, send one enquiry) and a form with product and quantity.
- **Certifications are a filter, not decoration.** Buyers in regulated industries use them to rule suppliers out, so they sit close to the top. When the site names a certification it appears in the hero strip and a standards section; when it does not, nothing is claimed.
- **Catalogue usability.** Search and filters beat long lists. Catalogue Pro and the WordPress catalogue design lead with search and category filters, and Lab Notes uses a live search over a spec table.
- **Technical documents next to products.** We do not host spec sheets, so product cards link to the site's own product page when it has one.
- **Application led navigation.** Many visitors arrive with a problem rather than a part number. "Industries we serve" and "Materials and uses" tiles come from the site's own cards when it has them.
- **Buyer guidance.** Each industry has a short checklist (ask for the safety data sheet, check the melt flow range, confirm the lead time) and a FAQ. These are general guidance and say so.

## What the clinic and hospital guides agree on
- **Dental:** a visitor decides in seconds whether the practice looks trustworthy, what it treats and how easy it is to book. Reviews, real photos and clear contact details matter; pricing transparency is called out as a gap on many sites. We show the Google rating and review count only when found, and never invent prices.
- **Hospitals:** a doctor or service finder close to the top, big shortcut tiles for booking, directions and calling, address, hours and phone prominent, and clear emergency information. Navigation by patient need, not by organisation chart. Care Compass has an emergency bar, a live treatment search with quick chips, and Book, Call and Doctors tiles. Emergency text appears only when the site gives it.
- **Kids and family, cosmetic, wellness, tech led dentistry** each want a different mood, so they got their own designs (Little Smiles, Luxe Aesthetic, Calm Breath, Glass Tech) rather than a recoloured one.

Industrial (electronics, plastic recycling, chemicals): Forge, Lab Notes, Closed Loop, Pellet Pop, Flow, Catalogue Pro, Plant Tour, Brand Lab (creative), Corporate WordPress, Catalogue WordPress, Elementor Style (plain). Clinics: Little Smiles, Luxe Aesthetic, Care Compass, Mono Clinic, Calm Breath, Glass Tech (creative) and Health Pro WordPress (plain). Every design has its own fonts, header, hero, product or treatment layout and signature effect; effects use transforms only, run for elements on screen, and switch off for reduced motion and on touch devices.

## Honesty rules (same recipe as before)
- Capacities, certifications, grades, client counts, prices, doctor names, hours, ratings and reviews appear only if the website or the supplied details contain them. Tests check that a thin profile claims no certification and no number.
- The prospect's own photos come first. Stock photos fill gaps and are captioned "Illustrative image". Stock for recycling avoids refinery and welding pictures.
- Copy has no dashes and no repeated lines; tests enforce both across every design.
